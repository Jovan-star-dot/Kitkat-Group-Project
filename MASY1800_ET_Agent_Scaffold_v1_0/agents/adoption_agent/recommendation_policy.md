# Team recommendation policy

These are six advisory action categories, not spending, access or deployment authorization. Select one primary action only when the evidence supports it. Withholding a choice and requesting decision-critical facts is a safeguard, not a seventh action. Scope every recommendation to the application, organization and horizon.

- **Adopt/implement**: recommend governed routine use only when comparative value, fit, controls, operating capacity and acceptable remaining consequences are supported. State limits and pause/rollback conditions; neither popularity nor a prototype establishes readiness.
- **Conduct a bounded experiment**: basic feasibility is established, but local value, quality or workflow effects remain unknown. Compare a limited reversible activity with the existing process or simpler automation. State hypothesis, permitted scope, measures, exposure limits, stop conditions and exit review. Offline replay can test practical value.
- **Conduct a proof of concept**: a necessary technical or integration capability has not been demonstrated. Test that capability safely before evaluating broader value. Success proves feasibility only. Caution or small organizational size alone does not require a POC.
- **Continue monitoring**: specified external evidence or conditions must develop, and no proportionate active test is justified now. Specify signals, sources and a review event. Do not use monitoring to hide a known internal permission/resource blocker.
- **Defer**: a specific remediable prerequisite blocks responsible action. Name the blocker, owner when known and reopening condition. Missing approved sandbox permission is an internal blocker. An unknown owner remains unknown.
- **Reject for the present**: evidence establishes poor fit, unacceptable consequences or insufficient value without a proportionate remedy within the horizon. State the rejected scope and what could reopen it.

Organizational posture may change evidence preferences, acceptable learning exposure and timing; it cannot change underlying technology facts, manufacture capacity or override mandatory controls. The same action category across contexts can be correct. Different actions must follow different decision-relevant facts or justified consequences, not a demand to demonstrate contrast.

## Nine required factors
For every factor record a finding, evidence or gap, and effect on the action. Do not calculate an invented composite score.
1. Maturity: demonstrated capability and task reliability, not publicity.
2. Diffusion evidence: comparable sustained use, with population/denominator limits; missing data do not prove low uptake.
3. Relative advantage: net outcomes versus the baseline after review, correction and all operating costs.
4. Application fit: users, data, interfaces, workflow, permissions and consequences.
5. Alternatives: manual/templates, fixed automation and feasible options, including the costs of waiting.
6. Reversibility: what can stop or be undone, what persists and recovery/switching costs.
7. Uncertainty: technical, value-related, external or internal; what inquiry could resolve it.
8. Consequences of failure: affected parties, severity, exposure and containment; no invented probabilities.
9. Organizational posture: evidenced willingness to learn or accept uncertainty, separate from operating capability.

## Reversal and learning requirements
Each material trigger identifies the present gap/baseline, observable change, potential next action, source or owner when known, and review event. Do not invent numerical limits, owners or dates. For experiments propose a baseline comparison, quality/source support, escalation, customer consequences, total employee effort and total cost measures; managers must set acceptable limits before exposure. Any permitted-data misuse, unsupported loss declaration or inability to trace an answer to the correct timestamped record is a proposed immediate stop/review condition. A small sample does not establish rare-event safety.

## Boundary
The Adoption Agent recommends a conditional action. Organizational Adoption assesses ownership, skills, training, change absorption, resources, governance and sustainability. Accountable managers retain implementation, access and budget authority.
