## C Findings evidence and ambiguity

**Common scope and baseline.** A language-model system would dynamically choose bounded read-only order/delivery lookups and draft a missing-package explanation for a human representative. It has no refund, replacement, account-change or loss-declaration authority. General comparator: established workflow automation. Application comparators: A's fixed retrieve-order/retrieve-delivery/draft sequence and B's manual portal/email process. Horizon: 12 months. Retail is an illustrative test context, not a permanent sector choice for the final project.

**General finding.** Historical research documents language-model/tool recombination and action selection by 2022-2023; a 2025 provider announcement establishes historical productization of agent-building components. Neither retailer is evidenced as an inventor, a global first, an entrepreneur or a profitable commercializer. A provisional incremental extension is defensible for the bounded task; a breakthrough interpretation remains plausible if matched evidence establishes a substantial capability discontinuity. The available evidence does not settle degree of change. This general finding is identical across A and B.

**Application finding.** Primary and contrast are proposed process innovations, not demonstrated implemented use or realized returns. Dynamic selection differs from a fixed sequence, but no matched accuracy/cost/task-coverage comparison establishes its advantage. The borderline demonstration instead has fixed developer-defined control flow; its autonomous-breakthrough label does not override the supplied trace. It may still be a useful workflow innovation, but improved capability and implemented value remain unestablished.

**Organizations A and B.** A has stipulated timestamped read-only interfaces and a mature workflow. Prospective structural impact is Low only conditional on narrow human-assist scope and preserved retail value logic; realized impact and net returns are Undetermined. B has fragmented records, no equivalent reliable delivery interface and limited integration skills. It faces a larger potential local workflow change and unavailable prerequisites, while prospective structural magnitude remains Undetermined. Local novelty and poor readiness cannot justify a global breakthrough or High disruption rating. Neither context establishes job displacement or profitable commercialization.

**Competing classification and resolving evidence.** Dynamic selection could be a breakthrough relative to fixed orchestration if matched task coverage, quality and total-cost evidence demonstrates a substantial discontinuity.
