# Organizational Readiness Framework

## Course basis and boundaries

The Assignment 7 brief asks whether an organization can absorb, govern, and sustain a proposed technology application. It requires consideration of leadership and sponsorship, skills, workflow, infrastructure and data, resources and budget, governance and risk, culture and change, and operating ownership.

Fortino, *Mastering the Craft of Emerging Technology Adoption*, Chapter 9, “Organizational Readiness and Implementation,” §§9.9–9.12, uses those eight dimensions and rates each as **Not ready**, **Partially ready**, or **Ready**. It identifies overall outcomes such as scaling now, building readiness first, or not scaling yet. It says a not-ready rating is not automatically a stop by itself; multiple not-ready dimensions may mean the organization is not ready to scale. It also directs the analyst to request information when evidence is too thin to rate a dimension.

Chapter 9 calls out a specific skills-and-training limitation in its own coverage. Apply the brief’s requirement to assess skills, but do not imply that the course supplies a detailed training design.

Fortino, Chapter 12, “Technology Adoption as a Journey,” contributes a complementary lens: translate technical capability into a specific organizational need and benefit; identify beneficiaries, likely incumbents or resisters, available allies or sponsors, and the sustained cost of carrying change through. Chapter 12’s personal-adopter instrument is not a substitute for the organization-wide readiness assessment in Chapter 9.

The rating labels above are course-provided. The additional operational labels and rules below are **proposed rules for this agent**, not instructor-defined thresholds.

## Assessment levels

Keep these findings distinct:

1. **Technology generally:** State only the relevant, supported technology-level finding, such as maturity. Do not use organizational limitations to lower the technology-maturity finding.
2. **Technology for the application:** Assess evidence about task fit and whether the application can plausibly perform the described work. Do not infer organizational readiness from technical fit.
3. **Application in this organization:** Assess the eight readiness dimensions using stated case evidence. A missing organizational fact is unknown, not a negative fact or a reason to change the general technology assessment.

If supplied evidence cannot support the technology or application finding, state that limitation rather than extending the readiness analysis into a broader adoption decision.

## Dimension ratings

Rate each dimension **Not ready**, **Partially ready**, or **Ready** only when the case supports a rating. If evidence is insufficient, write **Insufficient evidence to rate** and name the missing information. Do not treat “unknown” as “Not ready.”

For each dimension, record the supporting organizational evidence, strengths, gaps, effect on the ability to absorb/govern/sustain the application, any necessary condition before proceeding, and an accountable owner only if the case supports that attribution. Otherwise write **Owner unknown**.

| Dimension | Readiness questions and evidence to examine |
|---|---|
| Leadership and sponsorship | Is there a sponsor who permits evidence gathering and can support necessary decisions? Does sponsorship distinguish permission to investigate from a guarantee that the application should proceed? Is the support evidenced by a stated role, action, or commitment, rather than inferred from enthusiasm? |
| Skills and training | What specific skills are needed to operate, supervise, evaluate, or maintain this application? Which skills are demonstrated, and which are missing or unverified? Who, if anyone, is identified to address a gap? Do not assume training will resolve an unspecified capability gap. |
| Workflow and process | Is the organization’s current or reviewed workflow described well enough to assess where the application fits and where people retain responsibility? Are handoffs, exceptions, and process dependencies evidenced? A proposed application boundary or invitation for staff to describe exceptions is not proof that current procedures have been documented or validated. If case evidence does not establish actual or reviewed procedures, responsibilities, handoffs, exceptions, and dependencies, use “Insufficient evidence to rate” and ask for that evidence. Identify workflow gaps from the case; do not invent a process map or design a rollout. |
| Infrastructure and data | Are the required systems, interfaces, data access, quality, and data ownership described? Is the evidence specific to this organization? Distinguish an accountable data owner from a pilot or project lead when the case provides that information. |
| Resources and budget | Is there evidence of resources for more than a short pilot, including operating and usage-related costs where relevant? Is any production-scale funding explicitly authorized? Do not assume that pilot resources or enthusiasm establish a sustained budget. |
| Governance and risk | Are accountable roles and risk controls identified? Where relevant, does a human merely review outputs before action, or retain authority to override or stop operation? Are error impacts containable, and are safeguards proportionate to the case’s consequences? Do not give a legal conclusion without applicable jurisdiction and evidence. |
| Culture and change | What specific concerns, incentives, or likely resistance are supported by the case? Consider who benefits and who may bear costs. Distinguish legitimate concerns from protection of existing arrangements without assuming motive; both may coexist. Is there an evidenced response to the actual concerns, rather than only a generic communication plan? |
| Operating ownership | Who will be accountable for the application after the pilot or project team disbands? Is there a named ongoing business or operations owner and, where applicable, distinct owners for product, data, risk, and executive sponsorship? If the case does not identify them, state **Owner unknown**. |

## Overall readiness conclusion

Do not calculate a composite score, average the dimension ratings, or invent a numeric cutoff. The course does not prescribe a numeric aggregate threshold, and its description of how many “Not ready” dimensions preclude scaling is deliberately imprecise.

Use one of these **proposed operational overall labels**:

- **Ready to proceed to the next management gate:** Evidence supports the critical readiness dimensions; any remaining partial gaps have specific, supported conditions; and necessary ongoing ownership is identified or explicitly resolved. This is a readiness finding, not approval to adopt.
- **Build readiness first:** One or more material, potentially addressable gaps need to be closed before the organization can responsibly proceed. Name only actions or conditions tied to those gaps.
- **Not ready to proceed yet:** The case identifies a material barrier to absorbing, governing, or sustaining the application that is unresolved or cannot be bounded. Explain the evidence and what change would reopen the assessment.
- **Insufficient evidence to conclude:** One or more decision-critical dimensions cannot be rated from the supplied facts. Ask for specific evidence instead of converting the unknown into a readiness rating.

Use judgment about the consequence and interaction of gaps, not a tally. A serious governance or operating-ownership gap may matter more than several minor gaps; explain why it is decisive in the case rather than applying a generic rule.

## Prioritized readiness conditions

Prioritize only conditions that close an identified gap. For each, state:

- the gap and its case evidence;
- why it matters to the organization’s ability to absorb, govern, or sustain the application;
- what observable condition would close or reduce it;
- an owner or role only where supported by the case, otherwise **Owner unknown**; and
- what evidence should be supplied if the gap cannot yet be rated.

The agent may name the most consequential gap and the first readiness condition to resolve. It must not produce a generic checklist, complete implementation plan, or unsupported assignment of responsibilities.

## Required missing-information request

When a required fact is unknown, ask a specific question tied to a readiness dimension. Examples include:

- “Who is accountable for operating this application after the pilot team ends?”
- “Has a data owner confirmed the required data is accessible and fit for this use?”
- “What budget, if any, is authorized for continuing operation beyond the pilot?”
- “Which role can override or stop system actions, and what evidence shows that control works?”
- “What specific skills are available today, and who is responsible for closing any identified gap?”

Use only questions relevant to the actual uncertainty in the case. Do not fill in an answer based on what organizations commonly do.
