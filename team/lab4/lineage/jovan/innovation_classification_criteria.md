# Classification Criteria and Rating Guide

| Label | Decision rule | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Invention | A novel technical device, method, process, or concept is created. Invention alone does not establish use or value. | Original technical description, patent, research paper, prototype, or documented development. |
| Innovation | A new or meaningfully improved product, service, process, or business method is introduced or put into use and creates user or organizational value. | Demonstrated application, use, performance, adoption, or changed value proposition relative to a named baseline. |
| Entrepreneurship / commercialization | People or organizations mobilize resources, develop a market or delivery model, and bring an innovation to users at meaningful scale. | Product launch, market entry, business model, distribution, customer use, revenue, or scaling evidence. |
| Incremental innovation | Change improves an established product, process, or trajectory without substantially changing its underlying basis. | Before/after comparison showing bounded gains or extensions. |
| Breakthrough innovation | Change substantially alters feasibility, performance, or technical approach relative to a clear baseline. This describes degree of change and can coexist with the other labels. | Comparative technical evidence; claims must be scoped to a specific capability and baseline. |

These categories can overlap across different dimensions. The analysis must say what is being classified (technology development, application, or organizational use) and should not force one label to do all the work.

## Business-Model Disruption Judgment

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Low | Localized improvement; little change to how the organization creates or captures value. |
| Moderate | Material change to one or more activities or economics while the core model remains intact. |
| High | Meaningful pressure on the core value proposition, cost/revenue logic, channel, or competitive advantage. |
| Potentially Existential | A credible, evidence-based path could undermine the organization's ability to create or capture value. This is a possibility judgment, not a prediction. |

Require the analyst to name the affected current business-model element, explain the causal path, state what could be created and what could lose value, and identify an alternative interpretation or uncertainty.
