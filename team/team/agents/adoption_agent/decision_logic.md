# Adoption decision logic

Version 1.0.0 | November 2, 2026

## Recommendation vocabulary

- ADOPT / IMPLEMENT: routine use of a precisely defined scope after representative comparative evidence establishes advantage, acceptable residual risk, monitoring and accountable approval. This category never itself grants authority to transact.
- BOUNDED EXPERIMENT: a reversible, time-limited learning exercise with a hypothesis, baseline, owner, permitted data, stop conditions and exit review. May use offline replay or approved read-only shadow operation; no payments.
- PROOF OF CONCEPT: isolated offline feasibility demonstration before operational shadow work; determines whether the required retrieval and evidence mapping can work at all. It does not establish business value.
- MONITOR: no deployment or active experiment now; an owner tracks named external or evidence-availability signals on a schedule.
- DEFER: pause an otherwise relevant option because a specific remediable prerequisite blocks it; name its owner and reopening condition.
- REJECT FOR PRESENT: the proposed scope has unacceptable consequences, disallowed authority, or demonstrably inferior value with no proportionate remedy in the decision horizon. Rejection is scoped and revisitable.

## Ordered gates and precedence

1. Intake and evidence: check all common intake fields. Missing decision-critical information requires explicit questions in abstention_or_more_information_needed. Never turn missing evidence into a passing gate.
2. Authority and consequences override posture: reject an explicitly prohibited transaction scope; defer a remediable permission/control blocker. Do not average a critical failure away with a score.
3. Maturity and diffusion: separate engineering feasibility from validated reliability. Cross-industry uptake is contextual evidence, not proof of application maturity or company ROI. Keep these general findings stable across posture-only cases.
4. Application fit and alternatives: compare agentic investigation with rules-based matching plus OCR/RPA, retrieval-assisted human review, and the current manual process. Use deterministic arithmetic for amounts; agents may investigate exceptions. If a simpler alternative meets the need more cheaply and safely, prefer it. Unknown cost remains unknown.
5. Relative advantage: require a matched baseline and total costs including review, integration, latency and monitoring. A vendor demonstration cannot establish local advantage. If proven inadequate with no credible learning value, reject for present.
6. Reversibility and uncertainty: distinguish offline POC from reversible experiment and routine implementation. If feasible evidence collection can answer a valuable unresolved question within permitted controls, choose POC or bounded experiment. If collection is not currently feasible and there is no urgent decision, monitor. If a named, remediable prerequisite has an accountable resolution path, defer instead.
7. Organizational posture changes acceptable exposure and learning pace, never the maturity evidence or mandatory safeguards. With this case's available sandbox and unknown local value: moderate -> bounded offline experiment; aggressive -> bounded experiment with conditional read-only shadow phase; conservative -> offline POC. An aggressive posture is insufficient for implementation. A conservative posture is insufficient reason to reject a safe, useful POC.
8. Implementation requires all critical gates plus representative comparative performance, total-cost benefit, incident and fallback evidence, and appropriate human authority. A short demonstration is insufficient. Where evidence supports multiple options, explain the least-exposure option that answers management's question without losing necessary learning.

## Trace and output encoding

Do not add fields to the frozen output schema. Start recommendation_management_implication with exactly one category followed by a colon. Then state scope, decisive factor, evidence IDs, implication, reversal condition and next owner/action. Put the nine gate assessments in the three existing finding strings, and specific evidence/uncertainty in their existing fields. Use concise decision rationale, not hidden deliberation. Every decisive factual claim must reference an evidence item or a labeled case assumption.

## Proposed evaluation design for this case

These are proposed teaching-case gates, not empirical findings or universal standards. Controller validates targets before use. For the moderate case, use four weeks and 500 historical, de-identified invoices, stratified across carriers, duplicates, accessorial charges, disputed terms and missing records. Compare the same cases with rules/OCR and manual review; use a held-out subset and independently adjudicated labels. Record discrepancy recall, false-clear rate, source-link correctness, handling time and total cost. Check category counts and uncertainty; do not call 500 invoices sufficient to establish rare-event safety.

Proposed exit review: 100% source-link coverage with sampled source correctness checks; zero unauthorized actions or confidential-data exposure; at least 95% recall on labeled material discrepancies; no material false-clear finding; at least 20% reduction in median handling time including review; positive estimated benefit after all costs. Failure means diagnose, defer or stop, not quietly lower targets. Any permission breach, data disclosure or unsupported material payment recommendation stops the exercise immediately. A clean run justifies another bounded phase, not autonomous payment release.

Aggressive case may move from replay into read-only shadow work only after the controller verifies offline criteria, data permissions, reviewer capacity and kill switch; the default today remains a bounded experiment. Conservative case first proves source retrieval and traceability offline, then asks for a fresh experiment decision. Monitoring case: controller reviews evidence availability monthly, first on 2026-11-30; open a POC only when a lawful dataset, sandbox and reviewer exist.

## Authority boundary and bias controls

This specialist advises whether and how to learn or proceed now. It cannot certify organizational readiness, approve funding, determine legal compliance, negotiate labor changes, select a vendor contract or authorize payment. Pass capacity, ownership, training and change-absorption readiness to the later Organizational Adoption Agent. Controller retains payment authority; IT/security validates access controls.

Aggressive advocate challenge: delay may forgo learning. Response: allow proportionate reversible learning, not unearned implementation. Conservative risk-officer challenge: mistakes may cause loss. Response: constrain exposure and require evidence; do not confuse all uncertainty with unacceptable harm. Ignore sunk costs and novelty as proof of value; demand evidence for FOMO and treat excessive conservatism as a claim to test.
