# KitKat Weak-Output and Context-Contrast Review

Workshop date: September 29, 2026

Status: Constructed review exercise prepared with ChatGPT assistance.
These are hypothetical cases and illustrative responses, not outputs
from an executed specialist-agent test. Team review is pending.

## 1. Purpose

Identify generic analysis and establish how organizational context
should affect a recommendation while keeping general technology
evidence consistent.

## 2. Weak-output example

Technology: Agentic AI
Application: Customer-service support for missing packages

Deliberately weak response:
“AI agents improve customer service and reduce costs. The company
should adopt them to remain competitive.”

Why this response fails:
- It asserts benefits without supporting evidence.
- It ignores the specific workflow and organizational constraints.
- It does not address access to order and delivery systems.
- It omits uncertainty, alternatives, safeguards, and adoption costs.
- It recommends adoption without defining a test or success criteria.

Required improvement:
Connect the recommendation to the management question, cite evidence
for consequential claims, identify missing information, and explain
what conditions would justify a pilot, adoption, or deferral.

Preservation:
Keep this weak example alongside the critique and any later revised
response so reviewers can see what changed and why.

## 3. Context-contrast cases

Shared technology: Agentic AI
Shared application: Missing-package customer support
Shared management question: Should the organization pilot an agent
for this task?

Case A — Higher organizational readiness:
A hypothetical retailer has accessible order and tracking systems,
documented refund policies, a support team for escalations, and
resources to monitor a limited pilot.

Case B — Lower organizational readiness:
A hypothetical retailer has fragmented order records, inconsistent
tracking information, unclear refund policies, and limited staff
capacity to monitor errors.

These conditions are exercise assumptions, not researched findings
about actual companies.

## 4. Expected differences in analysis

For Case A:
Consider a limited, supervised pilot after verifying data quality,
permissions, and policy handling. Start with information retrieval
and draft responses. Keep consequential actions, such as refunds,
subject to defined approval rules.

For Case B:
Recommend addressing data access, policy clarity, and oversight
before an autonomous pilot. Consider a simpler workflow or a
staff-assistance tool while readiness gaps are resolved.

For both:
Do not treat an outdated tracking event as proof of loss. Identify
missing or conflicting information and escalate when necessary.

## 5. What should remain consistent

The underlying general technology evidence and its limitations
should remain consistent across both cases. Greater organizational
readiness does not itself prove that the technology is more capable.

Context should change feasibility, uncertainty, safeguards, timing,
and recommended action.

## 6. Review criteria

A satisfactory response must:
- Separate general, application, and organization-specific findings.
- Support consequential factual claims with traceable evidence.
- Distinguish supplied assumptions from verified facts.
- Explain how context changes the recommendation.
- Identify uncertainty and information needed before approval.
- Preserve accountable human judgment.
- Follow the common output contract when saved as an agent response.

A response fails this review if it gives both organizations the same
generic adoption recommendation without explaining their differences.
